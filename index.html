The problem

Testing one transport scenario should not take weeks.

Today, evaluating a scenario typically requires specialist modellers to manually build networks, configure simulations and interpret large volumes of output. It is slow and expensive, and it limits how many options planners can explore before committing to costly infrastructure decisions.

We are building an AI assistant that makes high-quality, evidence-based transport analysis faster and more accessible, so cities can evaluate more options, earlier.

What we do

From a planning question to an evidence-based answer.

Scenario modelling from plain language

Turn questions like "what happens if we close this lane during peak hours?" into structured simulation scenarios: network changes, demand assumptions and the performance metrics to evaluate.

Data preparation and checking

Clean and reconcile heterogeneous inputs, such as traffic counts, road network data, signal plans and planning documents, into consistent, validated model inputs.

Simulation and optimisation

Orchestrate our modelling and optimisation tools to run baseline and alternative scenarios, then iterate towards better-performing options.

Evidence-based reporting

Compare and explain results, summarising impacts on travel times, congestion, delays and network performance in clear reports for planners and government clients.

How it works

Ask. Model. Optimise. Explain.

  1. 1

    Describe the question

    A lane closure, a change to signal timing, a new residential development or a public transport route change, written in plain language.

  2. 2

    Scenarios and data are prepared

    The assistant interprets the request, translates it into structured scenarios, and prepares and checks the input data.

  3. 3

    Models run and improve

    Baseline and alternative scenarios run through our modelling and optimisation tools, iterating towards better-performing options.

  4. 4

    Results are compared and explained

    Impacts on travel times, congestion, delays and network performance are summarised into a clear, evidence-based report.

Modellers stay in the loop. Transport modellers, today the two founders, review assumptions and validate outputs at every step. The AI accelerates the work; the evidence is checked by people.

What an engagement looks like, from the first message to the decision. The interface shown is representative of the service, not a screenshot.

1 Ask

We are closing the kerbside lane on the corridor between A Street and B Street for six weeks of utility works, 7 to 10 am on weekdays. What happens to delays, and where does traffic divert?

I propose two scenarios against the AM-peak baseline: A, the closure as described, and B, the closure plus signal retiming at the three affected intersections. To set up the runs I need the current signal plans and recent counts on the two parallel streets. Do you have those?

Use cases

Questions we help answer.

Lane closures and roadworks

Quantify delay and diversion effects of temporary or permanent lane closures, by time of day.

Signal timing changes

Test and optimise signal plans across a corridor or a whole network before changing anything on the street.

New developments

Assess the traffic impact of residential or commercial developments and compare mitigation options.

Public transport changes

Evaluate route changes, frequency adjustments and priority measures and their effect on the wider network.

Network optimisation

Search across many candidate interventions to find options that perform best on the metrics that matter to you.

Policy and strategy questions

Turn policy documents and planning questions into testable scenarios with transparent assumptions.
